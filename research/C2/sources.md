# Primary-source audit — 2026-09-09

Novelty is NOT certified. The restricted compensation proofs are rebuilt in this package and do not invoke an external entropy-curvature theorem. Search covered DPP Shannon entropy concavity, projection-boundary Hessians, weak Fisher modes, likelihood identifiability, and principal-minor constraints. Failure to find an identical result does not prove absence.

1. [Brunel, Moitra, Rigollet, Urschel, Maximum likelihood estimation for discrete determinantal point processes](https://math.mit.edu/~urschel/publications/p2017e.pdf), pp. 4 and 8–9, equations (2.2)–(2.3), Theorems 8–9; their proof of the null-space theorem begins on p. 16. The L-ensemble law is the same standard algebra used and rederived here. Their Fisher null-space characterization concerns a full positive-definite L kernel and the expected log likelihood at a fixed data-generating law. C2 instead studies a rank-three observed L and selected low-event derivatives under A-affine change. Thus neither their likelihood concavity nor their null-space theorem supplies C2's full Shannon curvature sign. Relation: same Fisher/identifiability tools, not a checked implication of our restricted statement.

2. [Hino–Yano, Duality induced by an embedding structure of determinantal point process](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11024), pp. 6–8, Corollaries 1–2, Remarks 2–3, Lemma 2; proof of the corollaries on pp. 13–14. The quality-coordinate subfamily has a special exponential-family structure; the full Fisher matrix does not in general equal the Hessian of their potential. K supplies principal-minor expectation coordinates, which are nonlinear beyond singletons. This ruled out interpreting a weak Fisher mode as a free affine probability direction. No entropy sign theorem was imported.

3. [Mészáros, Limiting entropy of determinantal processes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11459), pp. 2, 8–9 and 19–21, conditional-entropy construction and Theorems 2.4–2.6. The random-order conditional-entropy representation supports a limit theorem under the paper's projection/tightness assumptions. Differentiating that representation in the present kernel parameter would still differentiate conditional probabilities and their weights. It was inspected as an alternative mechanism and supplies no immediate finite Hessian sign here.

4. [Al Ahmadieh–Vinzant, Characterizing principal minors of symmetric matrices via determinantal multiaffine polynomials](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13444), pp. 6–9, Theorems 3.1 and 3.5 with their algebraic proofs. Rayleigh-difference square conditions characterize the relevant determinantal representation over the specified unique factorization domain. They impose algebraic realizability, not the Shannon logarithmic sign. Our search therefore remained in actual A,V coordinates rather than optimizing free low-event probabilities.

5. [Yuzhou Gu, Entropy of Determinantal Point Processes, project report](https://sevenkplus.com/data/dpp.pdf), pp. 1 and 5–6, Corollary 6 and Theorem 7. The report gives a thinning endpoint inequality and rank-one affine-direction concavity. We inspected the rank-one proof: the event second derivatives vanish. C2's generic directions and pair-null direction have nonzero second derivatives, so this known mechanism does not settle them. This is an author-hosted course report, not treated as a comprehensive current status authority. No other claim from the report is used.

6. [Lyons–Steif, Stationary determinantal processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204324) is the task's historical stationary-problem source, not a theorem invoked in this finite-face proof. This round does not attack its entropy-rate limit and does not claim a stationary counterexample.

The above audit identifies classical ingredients and relevant distinctions. It does not certify an original paper-level contribution, and no claim of firstness is made.
