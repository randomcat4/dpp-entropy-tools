# Sources and actual use

Accessed 2026-09-09. Novelty is NOT_CERTIFIED; a targeted search failing to locate a theorem is not evidence that none exists. No external source is treated as proving B0.

1. Source public research snapshot: [PR #24](https://github.com/randomcat4/dpp-entropy-tools/pull/24), commit e988aa3003484f6368133b8bc0c668331629e369, tree e7c177ca74da59744443dbfcaadafd98724767ec. Read round2 verdict, checkpoint, provenance, beta-root proof/certificate, dense weak-edge proof, optimizer obstruction, and final nonauthor review; also the directly relevant earlier N-positive and pair-score proofs. The old root is calibration; old weak-edge and equal-soft-rate boundary results are not claimed as C1 discoveries. No full historical scan was repeated.

2. Hino–Yano, [An embedding structure of determinantal point process](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41884-024-00156-x), Information Geometry 7 (2024), 523–542; [arXiv v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11024v1). Examined Theorem 1, Corollary 1, the three-point example in §4.2 and the relevant proofs in §5. The quality parameters are exponential-flat directions. We directly reconstruct their full-score identity via diagonal L scaling, retaining the real cycle signs. That curved path supplies only the first-order K tangent; its flatness gives no K-affine entropy-curvature inequality. Our three mixed stationarity equations are a use of this structure, not a claim of a new exponential-family representation.

3. Al Ahmadieh–Vinzant, [Characterizing principal minors of symmetric matrices via determinantal multiaffine polynomials](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13444), Theorem 3.5, PDF p.9; proof and Dodgson identity in §3, PDF pp.6–9. Over a unique factorization domain, normalized multiaffine coefficients are symmetric principal minors iff the Rayleigh differences are squares. The field here is real, the empty minor is one, and our explicit K already realizes the minors. We directly expand the three-dimensional square. This is algebraic realizability, not an entropy sign theorem.

4. Mészáros, [Limiting entropy of determinantal processes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11459), §2.7 and Theorems 2.4–2.6 (PDF pp.8–9), §4 chain-rule proof (PDF pp.18–21), Theorem 5.1 (PDF p.21). Random-order conditional-entropy representations and graph convergence limits require the stated projection/tightness assumptions. They do not imply a finite three-point K-affine Hessian sign: conditional kernels and conditioning weights both vary with K. No interchange of an infinite limit and derivative is used here.

5. Lyons–Steif, [Stationary Determinantal Processes: Phase Multiplicity, Bernoullicity, Entropy, and Domination](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0204324), Conjecture 9.2, PDF p.53. This is the fixed-symbol stationary entropy question, contextual rather than a theorem usable for B0.

6. Yuzhou Gu, [Entropy of Determinantal Point Processes](https://sevenkplus.com/data/dpp.pdf), project report, introduction and rank-one Theorem 7. It explicitly distinguishes cardinality entropy from the full subset entropy and lists rank-one and thinning subcases. These do not cover the non-rank-one true M optimizer. We do not rely on its other claims or infer current global status solely from this report.
